What I wanted you to understand

My Baturi,

Last night, I wanted to watch a movie with you because it’s rare for me to suggest one, and I was looking forward to spending that time together. When you told me you were overwhelmed and feeling guilty about studying, I tried to reassure you that it’s okay to have difficult days and take a break. I wasn’t trying to pull you away from CAT prep. I’ve been encouraging you to study and giving you wake-up calls because I genuinely want to support you and see you do well. I thought a movie might help you unwind for a little while.

When you said you felt “suffocated,” I reacted by interrupting you to ask what you meant. I was confused and wanted to understand, not dismiss what you were saying. But I should have let you finish first, and I’m sorry I didn’t.

What hurt me was that after I apologised, we stopped talking and I couldn’t reach you. I know the word I interrupted you over may have meant something important to you, and I do want to understand it. At the same time, we both sometimes interrupt each other or misunderstand something in everyday conversations, and usually we apologise, clear it up, and move forward. I honestly didn’t expect this to leave us disconnected like this, and it’s been painful especially because I’ve only ever wanted to be someone who supports you, not someone who adds pressure.

I’m not saying this to minimise how you felt. I’m sorry for interrupting you, and I want to listen properly to what you were trying to tell me. I just hope we can give each other a little patience when things go wrong, and try to talk things through rather than letting one difficult moment come between us.

I love you, Baturi. I want to understand your side, and I hope you can understand mine too. We don’t have to see every moment the same way to be gentle with each other.

Love,
your Bhatura ♡
